          Sources

            Read with care. Totals add up only what each site reports; many large oil fields, including Saudi Arabia's, publish no production. The US critical-mineral set is far more detailed than the global one, so the US shows many more deposits. Solar, wind, hydro and nuclear come from a 2021 database. Refinery capacities are nameplate figures from Climate TRACE; a few missing ones use the country median. Gas is converted at 6,000 cf per barrel of oil equivalent. Country totals count operating sites only; offshore sites go to the nearest country. Country water stress is an area-weighted average of Aqueduct basin scores. Borders follow Natural Earth's de facto lines and don't imply endorsement.

            Live layers. Fires, conflict reports, ships and aircraft are snapshots refreshed every 30 minutes or so by the site's build, not a live stream; each layer shows when it was taken. Conflict reports are machine-coded from news articles and are often duplicated, misplaced (to a country's centre, say) or wrong; open the linked articles before relying on one. A fire next to a conflict report isn't proof of an attack. Warship positions come from USNI News's weekly fleet tracker, which names a port or sea area rather than coordinates, so they're approximate and only cover publicly reported US Navy ships; other navies rarely publish positions and their warships usually switch AIS off. Worldwide merchant ships need an aisstream.io key in the build; without one the ship layer shows the Baltic, live. Business jets cover about 35 common jet types and airliners about 60 passenger, regional and freighter types, not every plane; aircraft whose owners have opted out of public tracking (the FAA's LADD list and privacy ICAO addresses) are left out, and no owner names are shown. Search a registration or callsign to find one. Military bases combine Wikidata and OpenStreetMap and are still incomplete, so a gap doesn't mean there's no base. Cable routes are schematic.
